### Near-Inertial Wave Generation

Near-inertial waves are a primary mechanism by which wind energy at the surface of the ocean reaches the interior, mixing the deep ocean. They are generated by horizontal divergences in the inertial oscillations that resonant wind stress drives in the mixed layer, but the fraction of wind work that goes into near-inertial wave energy is poorly constrained, and submesoscale vorticity gradients can enhance the vertical energy flux through refraction. During the Task Force Ocean experiment in November 2020, we observed the generation of near-inertial waves by a storm with a novel array of five drifting Wirewalker profilers, together with a drifting thermistor chain. Despite coherent wind forcing on 100 km scales, the near-inertial wave field varies significantly on 10 km scales. Downward-propagating wave energy is enhanced down the horizontal vorticity gradient across the array, and upward-propagating energy is enhanced in the upper thermocline. The array observations allow an inertial kinetic energy budget of the mixed layer to be constructed, constraining the ratio of wind work to near-inertial wave generation.
